## Why Nanjing is worth two or three days

Nanjing (南京, "southern capital") is a former imperial capital on the Yangtze River in Jiangsu Province, west of Shanghai. A lot of its history is still standing. About 25 km of the early-Ming city wall survives, the first Ming emperor's tomb sits on Purple Mountain, and the old Confucian exam district along the Qinhuai River is now a lively, lantern-lit place to spend the evening. High-speed trains connect Nanjing to Shanghai, Beijing, Hangzhou and Suzhou, so it fits easily into a wider East China trip. Two to three days covers the highlights.

## Top things to do in Nanjing

- **Sun Yat-sen Mausoleum (中山陵):** 392 stone steps up the southern slope of Purple Mountain to a memorial hall with a blue-glazed roof. Entry is free but you must reserve, and it is closed on Mondays (with holiday exceptions).
- **Ming Xiaoling (明孝陵):** the tomb of Zhu Yuanzhang, founder of the Ming dynasty, added to UNESCO's World Heritage List in 2003. Walk the Sacred Way, lined with stone animals.
- **City Wall and Zhonghua Gate (中华门):** construction of the wall began in 1366. The gate has three barbicans and 27 vaulted chambers for troops.
- **Xuanwu Lake (玄武湖):** a former imperial lake with five islands, free to enter, right next to Nanjing Railway Station.
- **Nanjing Museum (南京博物院):** founded in 1933. The permanent galleries are free with a reservation, and it is closed on Mondays.
- **Memorial Hall of the Victims in the Nanjing Massacre:** a solemn site. Plan two to three quiet hours here.
- **Qinhuai River at night:** lanterns and boat cruises around the Confucius Temple (Fuzimiao).

The [See & Do page](https://nanjing.yuzicheng.org/see-and-do/) lists the nearest metro station for each sight.

## What to eat in Nanjing

Nanjing food is mild and savoury rather than spicy, and duck is at the centre of it. Start with **salted duck (盐水鸭)** and **duck blood vermicelli soup (鸭血粉丝汤)**. Then try **soup dumplings (汤包)**, **beef potstickers (牛肉锅贴)** and the osmanthus sweets, especially **桂花糖芋苗**, taro in osmanthus syrup. The easiest places to find them are the Fuzimiao area, Laomendong, and the Shiziqiao food street off Hunan Road. The [Nanjing food guide](https://nanjing.yuzicheng.org/eat/) gives the Chinese name of every dish, so you can show it to staff when you order.

## How to book attractions, trains and taxis

China runs on reservations and QR codes. The big sights admit visitors by name, so even the free ones need a timed booking made with your **passport**. You usually do this in each attraction's official WeChat mini-program: search for its Chinese name, tap 预约, choose Passport (护照) as the ID type, and add a separate entry for each person. Bring the original passport on the day. You can buy train tickets on the official 12306 English site or app, or through agencies such as Trip.com. Check whether your ticket says Nanjing South or Nanjing Station. The easiest way to get a taxi is DiDi inside Alipay or WeChat. The step-by-step [booking guide](https://nanjing.yuzicheng.org/how-to-book/) walks through each of these.

## How to use WeChat in China as a foreigner

Set up payments at home, before you fly.

1. **Register WeChat** with your own mobile number. It must be able to receive SMS, and more than 200 countries and regions are supported. If WeChat asks for assisted registration, an existing user scans your QR code. If you don't have a helper, WeChat offers card-based verification instead. Don't use a VPN or an unofficial app while you register.
2. **Link a Visa or Mastercard** under Me → Pay and Services → Wallet → Bank Cards. According to WeChat's help centre, you don't need a +86 number, transactions under ¥200 are fee-free and a 3% fee applies above that, and foreign cards can't be used for red packets or transfers.
3. **Add Alipay** too. It covers the metro ride code and DiDi.
4. **Carry some cash** in small notes as a backup.

You can find the details in the [WeChat & payments setup guide](https://nanjing.yuzicheng.org/wechat-and-payments/).

## A classic base: the Jinling Hotel

The **Jinling Hotel (金陵饭店)**, at 2 Hanzhong Road in Xinjiekou, opened on 4 October 1983. Its 37-storey tower, about 110 m tall, was the tallest building in China at the time, and it had the country's first high-rise revolving restaurant. Xinjiekou metro station (Lines 1 and 2) is right below the square, and Line 2 runs straight to the Museum, the Mausoleum and the Memorial Hall. The [Jinling Hotel page](https://nanjing.yuzicheng.org/jinling-hotel/) covers its history and its restaurants.

## FAQ

**How many days do I need in Nanjing?** Two to three days covers the main sights.

**Which attractions close on Mondays?** The Sun Yat-sen Mausoleum, the Nanjing Museum and the Memorial Hall normally close on Mondays, except on national holidays.

**Do I need WeChat in Nanjing?** It's strongly recommended, because many reservations go through WeChat mini-programs. For payments alone, Alipay works well.

**Do I need a visa?** It depends on your nationality. As of October 2026, ordinary-passport holders from 50 countries can enter visa-free for up to 30 days, and 57 nationalities can use 240-hour visa-free transit through Nanjing Lukou Airport. Confirm with official sources before you travel.
